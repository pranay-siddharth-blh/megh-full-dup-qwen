1. What is the Meghalaya Tourism Homestay Scheme?
The Meghalaya Tourism Homestay Scheme, scaled up as the **Chief Minister’s Meghalaya Homestay Mission (CMMHM)**, is a state government program run by the **Tourism Department** in collaboration with the **District Commerce and Industry Centres (DCIC)** and partner banks. It provides up to **70% subsidy** (and up to **80% for PwD applicants**) on homestay construction and renovation projects to promote community-led, sustainable eco-tourism.

2. What is the target of the Chief Minister's Meghalaya Homestay Mission?
The Mission aims to set up **3,000 standardized homestays** and create more than **15,000 direct and indirect jobs** across the state by **2028**.

3. What are the three main components of the scheme?
1. **Component 1 (New Homestays):** Construction of new standardized rural homestays (project cost up to ₹10 lakh).
2. **Component 2 (Upgradation):** Financial assistance to upgrade existing, operational homestays (up to ₹2 lakh).
3. **Component 3 (Boutique Homestays):** Support for 10+ room boutique accommodation facilities (minimum ₹80 lakh investment).

4. How much subsidy is provided for building a new homestay?
* **General / Special (ST/SC/Women) Categories:** Up to **70%** of the approved project cost (up to ₹7,00,000 on a ₹10,00,000 project).
* **Persons with Disabilities (PwD) Category:** Up to **80%** subsidy, capped at a maximum of **₹8,00,000**.

5. How does the 70% subsidy breakdown work?
The 70% subsidy is achieved by combining two funding sources:
* **35% subsidy** under the Central Government’s **Prime Minister’s Employment Generation Programme (PMEGP)** through DCIC.
* **35% top-up subsidy** provided directly by the **Tourism Department, Government of Meghalaya**.

6. What is the beneficiary's own financial contribution (promoter equity)?
The beneficiary must contribute only **5% of the project cost** (for ST/SC/Women/Special Category entrepreneurs) or **10%** (for General category). The remaining 90%–95% is initially covered through a bank loan supported by the government subsidy.

7. What are the repayment and moratorium terms for the bank loan?
* **Moratorium:** Beneficiaries receive a **1-year (12-month) moratorium** from the date of initial disbursement, during which no principal repayment is required.
* **Tenure:** Up to **7 years (84 months)** total repayment period.
* **Effective EMI:** Because the Tourism Department pays its 35% subsidy as direct EMI assistance during the first 2–3 years, the owner's net repayment averages only ~**₹6,000 per month**.

8. Can I apply for a project cost higher than ₹10 lakh under Component 1?
Yes, you can apply for projects up to **₹20 lakh** under PMEGP credit linkage. However, the state Tourism Department’s 35% subsidy is capped at a maximum of **₹3.5 lakh** (35% of ₹10 lakh). Any loan amount beyond ₹10 lakh must be serviced under standard bank loan terms without additional government subsidy.

9. What financial assistance is available for upgrading an existing homestay (Component 2)?
Existing homestay owners can receive a grant of **₹50,000 per room**, up to a maximum ceiling of **₹2,00,000 (Rupees Two Lakhs)** per homestay.

10. What are the eligibility rules for the Upgradation Grant (Component 2)?
To qualify for the ₹2 lakh upgradation grant, an applicant must:
1. Have operated the homestay for **at least 6 continuous months**.
2. Have an official guest register showing at least **50 bed-nights**.
3. Be registered and listed on the **Meghalaya M-TIME Portal**.

11. What is the Boutique Homestay Scheme (Component 3)?
Component 3 supports investors developing boutique accommodation with **at least 10 rooms** and a minimum capital investment of **₹80 lakh**. Financed on a 90:10 debt-equity model, the government provides up to **₹15 lakh in incentives** (₹10 lakh in 12-month EMI assistance + ₹5 lakh National Games Accommodation Bonus for eligible clusters in Shillong, Tura, and Jowai).

12. Who is eligible to apply for the standard homestay scheme?
* Must be an Indian citizen and a **permanent resident/domicile of Meghalaya**.
* Must be at least **18 years old** and functionally literate.
* Must **not be a defaulter** with any bank or financial institution.
* Must own legal land or hold a registered lease agreement for at least **7 years**.
* Only **one person per family** is eligible for assistance under the scheme.

13. Can more than one member of the same family get the subsidy?
**No.** The guidelines state that only one member per family/household can receive financial assistance under the scheme.

14. What are the "33 Approved Standard Designs"?
To preserve indigenous aesthetics, guarantee structural safety, and maintain high hospitality standards, all new homestays must be constructed using one of the **33 standardized architectural layouts** designed and approved by the Tourism Department.

15. Can I use my own custom architectural blueprint instead of the 33 designs?
**No.** For standard homestays under Component 1, selecting one of the 33 approved designs is mandatory. This ensures high structural standards, speeds up bank approvals, and eliminates private architect costs.

16. What documents are required for land proof?
* **For Landowners:** Land Ownership Document (Title Deed, Patta, or Land Holding Certificate).
* **For Leaseholders:** A valid, legally registered Lease Agreement with a minimum unexpired period of **at least 7 years** (15 years for boutique homestays).
* **Local Authority NOC:** A valid No Objection Certificate from the local **Rangbah Shnong / Dorbar Shnong** (in Khasi/Jaintia Hills) or the **Nokma** (in Garo Hills).

17. What personal identification documents do I need to apply?
* Voter ID (EPIC) or Aadhaar Card.
* PAN Card.
* Recent passport-size photographs.
* Caste / ST / Disability Certificate (to claim special or PwD subsidy rates).
* First page of the applicant's bank passbook or a cancelled cheque.

18. Where and how do I apply for the homestay scheme?
Applications are submitted **online** via the Meghalaya Investment Promotion Authority portal (`https://invest.meghalaya.gov.in`) or through the citizen services portal (`https://meghalayaone.gov.in`). Offline paper forms are not accepted.

19. Do I need hospitality training to run the homestay?
**Yes.** Selected beneficiaries must complete mandatory hospitality training covering front-office management, housekeeping, hygiene, and local culinary skills organized by the Department of Tourism and the Institute of Hotel Management (IHM), Shillong.

20. Which banks provide loans under this scheme?
The primary partner bank is **Meghalaya Rural Bank (MRB)**, alongside State Bank of India (SBI) and other participating commercial banks coordinated through the State Level Bankers' Committee (SLBC).

21. How is the loan money disbursed?
Loan funds are disbursed directly by the bank in **construction-linked installments** based on physical verification of work by District Tourism Officers and engineers.

22. What is the Meghalaya M-TIME Portal?
**M-TIME** (Meghalaya Tourism Information & Management Engine) is the official digital tourism portal and app where all homestays, hotels, and guides are registered, allowing tourists to verify and book authenticated homestays.

23. Can I convert the homestay into a private home or rental flat later?
**No.** The property must operate as a registered tourist homestay. If a beneficiary converts the property into private housing, sells it, or stops tourist operations during the loan tenure, the government will initiate subsidy recovery and legal proceedings.

24. What happens if an applicant defaults on the loan?
If an entrepreneur willfully defaults on their loan obligations, the state government and the financing bank can revoke the subsidy and initiate legal recovery under the Meghalaya Public Demands Recovery Act.
