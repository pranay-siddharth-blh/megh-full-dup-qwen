What this plan does and does not guarantee

  • Guaranteed: no task waits for a task in another lane. I moved or split six tasks to make that true, and removed four dependencies that turned out to be merge order only. The section "What changed versus the audit" lists every change.
  • Not guaranteed: zero merge conflicts. Seven files are touched by two lanes in separate regions (table below). Git normally merges these on its own. If it cannot, the rebase rule in each prompt tells the session how to resolve it without waiting.
  • Outside blockers still exist: the VPN, SMEs, the data team, the infra team and your decisions. They never stop a lane: the session does every part it can, records the blocked part, and moves to its next task.
  • Each merge reaches Main, and your CI/CD may deploy it. Risky changes ship switched off: retention purge disabled until you pick a period, superuser routing not done until the DB team agrees, and the boot guard active only when ENV=prod.
